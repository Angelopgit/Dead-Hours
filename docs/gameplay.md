# Gameplay systems

## The map

Vesper, 250 × 300 m (data in `shared/src/map/maple-hollow.ts`): Hale Street, Pell Lane and Kinney Street (the next street over: ten more enterable homes, five with basements, tee-ing into Pell and dead-ending at a trail from the park) with sidewalks, alleys and fourteen streetlights (a few dead, one flickering); ten houses on the first two streets (eight enterable homes, four with basements, the two-storey safehouse with kitchen, living room, three bedrooms, basement, garage and four ways in, and the enterable Mercer house); the Rudy’s gas station with an enterable store; a park with playground and gazebo; a police checkpoint with a barrier chicane and floodlights; fenced backyards with sheds and tight side passages; woods around the edge.

## Combat

Solo play runs the same authoritative simulation the game server runs online (`shared/src/sim`). The client only sends intents (fire along the camera ray, reload, switch, melee, bandage, use) and renders the events that come back, so damage, ammunition and health never live in the client.

- Weapons (`shared/src/config/weapons.ts`): to start with, the M4A1 assault rifle, MP5 submachine gun, M870 pump shotgun (shell-by-shell reload, interruptible) or MK14 marksman rifle (penetrates one body), with the M17 pistol (or two of them) and a machete. Supply drops bring the M249 SAW, SCAR-H, M107 .50 cal, an RPG-7 and a suppressed .338 bolt-action; houses hide a katana, bat, crowbar, pipe wrench and hockey stick. Pick the starting primary in Loadout, swap it at the garage weapon table.
- Ammunition is limited. Ammo crates refill reserves twice each; first aid boxes hold bandages (Q, 2.6 s, +40 HP, interrupted by damage).
- Zombies have head, torso, arm and leg hit zones (head ×2.5). They hear gunfire and footsteps, spot you in their sight cone (crouching shortens it), path through houses, pound and break doors, stagger and get knocked down.
- 45 seconds of preparation, then the outbreak: a growing population, with horde waves announced by a siren (the first at 2:30, the next 2 minutes 40 seconds after it, each gap 12 seconds shorter than the last down to 2 minutes; `HORDES` in `shared/src/config/zombies.ts`) and the van raid once a night, just after the first downtime. Death ends a solo run with the results screen.
- Downtime (`LULLS` in `shared/src/config/zombies.ts`, `shared/src/sim/Lulls.ts`): after every five minutes or so of fighting the dead fall back for one to two minutes (60 s the first time, 15 s longer each time after, up to 2 minutes). It is announced with a countdown in the timer, a supply drop comes in, no zombies spawn, idle ones far off and out of sight slip away, and only those already hunting someone keep coming; a warning goes out 15 seconds before the end and the spawner climbs back over 45 seconds after. A lull that falls due during a horde siren or surge waits for the wave to be over, and none starts while the van raid is on. The night's threat clock (spawn growth, the zombie mix, horde waves and the raid) stands still during a lull, so the fight picks up where it left off. Online, every survivor who died is brought back at the start of each lull, beside a teammate still standing, with full health and their guns loaded; their run counts on to their final death, less the time they were dead. Check with the "Downtime lulls" section of `npm run check:sim`.
- Gore can be set to Off, Reduced or Full in Settings → Graphics.
- Gun shots are real recordings (`client/src/audio/recorded`, CC0 from Freesound, see the sound credits in the README), four takes per gun picked at random with slight pitch variation: the M17 and MP5 from a 9 mm pistol at an outdoor range (the MP5's takes shorter and barkier), the M4A1 and M249 from an AR-15 (5.56, the M249 a touch lower), the MK14 from a .303 Lee-Enfield (no clean, freely licensed 7.62 NATO recording was found; it is the nearest full-power rifle), the SCAR-H from a SCAR-H, the M870 from a 12 gauge fired four times, the M107 from a Barrett .50 in an open field and a .50 BMG at a range. They are Freesound's high-quality previews, not the original files (those need a login). `client/dev/make_recorded_sounds.py` rebuilds them: it finds each shot, cuts and pitches the takes, adds body and bark with EQ, limits, and checks they decode under full scale. Your own shot adds a sub-bass kick and pushes the rest of the mix down for a moment. Guns play on their own bus, louder than other effects and following the Effects volume, with no compressor on it (one flattened each crack by 15 dB); a soft clipper on the output rounds off anything over the top. Until the recordings are decoded, or if they fail to, the synthesized shots from `client/src/audio/GunSynth.ts` play (muzzle blast, gas jet, supersonic crack, steel ring); the action under each shot and the dry fire are still synthesized.
- Reloads are real recordings too (the Free Firearm Sound Library, see the sound credits in the README), placed on each gun's hand animation (`client/src/weapons/reloadScript.ts`): the M4A1's release clicks and the magazine scrapes out as the hand takes it, goes into the pouch, a fresh one comes out and seats with a hard click as the hand pushes it home. A reload from empty also sends the bolt home (the bolt catch on the M4A1 and SCAR-H, the op rod on the MK14, the charging handle on the M107 and M249, the slide stop on the M17); with a round still chambered it doesn't. The MP5 locks its bolt back before the magazine comes out and slaps it home after; the M249 opens its feed cover, swaps the box, lays the belt and slams the cover; the M17's magazine drops free and clatters at your feet; the M870 thumbs each shell into the tube as the hand reaches the port and racks the pump after loading an empty tube. Each take leads up to its main hit and the engine lines that hit up with the hand. Other survivors' reloads come from their hands, carry about 30 m, are muffled through walls, and ring in the room they are in, like your own.
- Hits on zombies are heard, not beeped: the hit marker is visual only, and every round going into a body plays a recorded flesh impact with a wet layer, a skull hit on headshots and a heavy punch under big or melee hits, from where it landed (late by distance, muffled through walls). Your own hits carry farther than other people's; a load of buckshot lands as one hit.
- Gore and bodies (`shared/src/config/gore.ts`): how hard a kill throws the body depends on the gun (m/s at the body's centre: SMG 1.7, pistol 1.9, M4A1 2.5, M249 2.7, SCAR-H 3.6, MK14 4.4, M870 5.6 within 5 m falling to 30% at 16 m, M107 7.2; brutes and bloaters are thrown less), and the big guns lift the body off its feet, so a pistol drops a zombie where it stands and a shotgun or a .50 knocks it flat on its back a couple of metres away. Most of an M870 volley up close also shoves a living zombie back (anything short of a brute). A killing head shot from the SCAR-H, MK14 or M107, or two buckshot pellets in the face up close, blows the head apart: red mist, pieces of skull with scalp on them, brain and meat thrown along the shot, splatter on whatever is behind and a neck that pumps blood. The simulation decides throws, shoves and head pops and sends them with the kill, so everyone online sees the same death. Every hit throws real 3D pieces of meat (heavy rounds take bone too) that tumble, bounce off walls and the street, leave a wet mark where they land and lie there for half a minute; heavy rounds also blow a jet of blood and a red mist out of the back, paint a longer splatter behind and let blood fall onto the ground under a kill. Shot-up zombies drip blood as they walk, and pools spread wider under the dead. Bodies thud when they hit the ground, land on and roll off the bodies already lying there, jerk and bleed when shot again (every player's rounds, so all screens see it), are thrown about by grenades and bursting bloaters, and get their limbs kicked aside when you walk through them. Heavy rounds land with a thicker wet layer and a thump; a head coming apart cracks and splatters, and the pieces pat and click as they come down. Gore Off throws no pieces and pops no heads; Phone and Low graphics keep fewer pieces for a shorter time.
- Zombies carry farther than other sounds (full level to 6 m, then fading slowly, so a groan is still heard at 60 m) and are muffled through walls. Their footsteps are heard to 45 m: walkers plant a heel and drag the other foot, runners slap the ground fast, brutes stomp; floorboards sound hollow, a basement hard. Distant steps share a budget so a horde doesn't turn into noise.
- Where a shot is heard decides how it sounds (`client/src/audio/Acoustics.ts`). Raycasts against the map sort each spot into street, room, hall (garages and the store, by their concrete floors) or basement, and each space has its own echo: on the street the facades slap a shot back one by one for about two seconds, a living room rings short and dull (0.8 s), a garage or the store longer (1.1 s), the concrete basement booms (1.5 s). A shot fired indoors also leaks, muffled, into the street echo, and a street shot leaks into the room you are in.
- Every shot that is not your own plays from where it was fired with 3D (HRTF) panning, late by its distance at 343 m/s and with less of its direct sound the farther it is. Walls between you and the gun muffle it by what they are made of (glass a little, wood and plaster a lot, brick and concrete almost entirely; `Physics.crossings` lists every surface on the line). A supersonic round passing within 10 m cracks from the point where it passed you, ahead of the report: loud for the M4A1 and MK14, faint for 9 mm and buckshot, none for the M17. At most 10 shots from others play at once; a nearer one takes over from the farthest. Online, other survivors' shots go through this same path from their positions. Inside, a recorded take's own outdoor tail is cut short and the room's echo takes over.
- To hear or measure the gun sound, run `npm run dev` and open `/dev/gunclips.html`: **Render clips** plays each gun in each space, the distance, wall and fly-by cases, flesh hits, zombie steps and groans through the game's own audio graph, with a save link per clip (`renderClips({ synth: true })` from the console renders the synthesized shots instead). `python3 -I client/dev/analyze_gunclips.py CLIPDIR OUTDIR` measures saved clips (C50, ring time, spectral centroid, crack and report timing), draws spectrogram sheets and joins each gun's four spaces into one A/B file.
- To stress the sound in a four-player firefight, open `/dev/firefight.html`: **Play it live** fires three teammates on full auto and your bursts into a horde (flesh, impacts, cases, steps, groans) through the real engine and reports dropped or cut teammate shots and audio underruns (`AudioContext.playbackStats`). From the console, `firefight({ seconds: 10, wav: true })` renders it offline instead and reports the audio thread's load second by second; a load that climbs over the fight means finished sounds are not leaving the mix.
- Barricading: from inside a home, hold G at a door or ground-floor window to nail boards across it (4 on a door, 3 on a window, 1.5 s each; the hammering carries to zombie ears). Hold E to pry one off. A boarded door won't open. Zombies beat the boards off one at a time, smash window glass and climb in through empty windows; doors still get pounded until they burst. Bullets shatter panes and fly on into the room, and shots into a closed door splinter its boards, then knock it in. Everything is decided by the simulation and synced to every player online.
- Aiming looks down the sights in first person: the camera moves into the survivor's eye and the weapon's sights line up with it. Out of the box the M4A1 has iron sights, the MP5 a peep sight and hooded post, the M870 a fiber-optic bead, the M17 three-dot night sights and the MK14 a 6× scope (zoomed view with a duplex reticle); fitted optics replace them (see Progression). Settings → Controls → Aim view switches back to the over-the-shoulder zoom.
- Skateboards (`shared/src/sim/Skateboards.ts`, `client/src/gameplay/Skateboards.ts`): six boards lie on the streets, sidewalks and driveways each night (one a short walk from the safehouse, the rest spread out, some flipped wheels-up), in the same spots for everyone in a room. Walk up and press E (RB on a controller, USE on a touch screen) to hop on and again to step off. Forward pushes (Shift pushes harder: about 10 m/s after two seconds and up to 12.5, against 6 on foot; the view widens with speed), A/D carve, back brakes, Space ollies. Hold a direction as you ollie for a flip trick: left kickflips, right heelflips, back pop shove-its, back-left 360 flips and back-right varial heelflips (the left stick or the touch stick with A or JUMP on a controller or a phone). The board flips and spins under the rider's tucked knees, the front foot flicks it (or the back foot scoops a shove-it round), both feet catch it, and the trick's name is called out when you land it (a streak counts tricks landed within three seconds of each other). Everyone else in the room sees your tricks: the server passes each one on and their browser plays it as you leave the ground. Grass and dirt grab the wheels. Shooting and aiming work as usual while rolling (spread grows a little less with speed than on foot), and the camera swings in behind the board unless you look around. The wheels grind over asphalt, hiss on smooth concrete and clack over every sidewalk seam; kicks, ollie pops, flicks and catches, landings, hopping on and off and bails all have their own sounds, from your board and everyone else's (synthesized in `AudioEngine`; render them with `/dev/skateclips.html` beside a survivor's running steps). Zombies hear the wheels at a few metres; a hard hit or running into a wall fast throws you off. Online, each rider's browser moves the board like it moves their feet: the heading rides in the motion message, the server lets riders go faster than runners, and snapshots carry who is on which board. Check with `npm run check:skate`.
- Bicycles (`shared/src/sim/Bicycles.ts`, `client/src/gameplay/Bicycles.ts`, tuning in `shared/src/config/bicycles.ts`): four bikes lie on the streets each night (one a short walk from the safehouse), in the same spots for everyone in a room. Press E (RB on a controller, USE on a touch screen) beside one to get on and again to get off; it falls over where you leave it. Forward pedals (about 10.5 m/s, Shift stands on the pedals for 13.5), back brakes (hard at speed skids the back tyre), A/D steer (the bike leans into turns), Space bunny hops. Both hands are on the bars, so the rider can't aim or shoot: the fire button rings the bell instead (heard down the street, zombies included). Online, a second survivor can walk up to a teammate's bike while it is slow and press E to hop on the pegs over the back wheel: they ride along and can aim and shoot freely while the rider pedals (a passenger makes the bike a little slower and too heavy to hop). A hard hit knocks either of them off, and riding into a wall fast throws both. The tyres, the freewheel ticking while coasting, sidewalk seams, skids, landings, the bell, getting on and crashes are synthesized in `AudioEngine` (render them with `/dev/bikeclips.html`). Online the rider's browser moves the bike, the server puts the passenger on the pegs every step, and snapshots carry who rides and who stands on the pegs. Check with `npm run check:bike`.

## Night goals and the first night

Each night has three team goals (`shared/src/sim/Contracts.ts`). Boarding up is always the first; the other two are picked from the night's seed out of head shots, kills up close, torn-off arms, screamers, brutes, hordes held off, supply drops emptied, the raiders and kills with grenades or fire, so the server and every client agree on them. They are counted from the simulation's own events, their targets grow with the team when the outbreak begins (`perPlayer` in `GOALS`), and finishing one gives everyone in the night a goal on their stats, worth `XP.goal` (150) each in `runXp`. Online, progress goes out in each snapshot as `ct` and the client's copy only mirrors it. The card at the top left of the HUD lists them (phones get the GOAL DONE notices only).

A player's first two nights (`client/src/gameplay/FirstNight.ts`, kept in local storage) swap the card for three steps before they come: board a window, pick up supplies, check your gear.

## Progression

Every night earns XP (`shared/src/config/progression.ts`): time survived, kills, headshots, arms torn off, arm kills, supplies found and night goals. Levels run 1 to 50 and each one opens attachments and finishes for the guns you can start with (not the machete or the supply-drop guns).

- Optics (red dot, holographic, 3× prism, 6× scope), muzzle devices (suppressor, compensator, full choke), underbarrel (vertical grip, angled grip, laser), magazines (extended, fast) and eleven finishes. Each attachment changes the gun's numbers both ways, for example a suppressor cuts the noise zombies hear by 60% but costs damage and range. Finishes are cosmetic.
- Main menu → **Loadout** is the armory: a 3D gun on a turntable with clickable fitting labels, the options for each slot (locked ones say the level they open at), what each change does, and the unlock track.
- In a night, press **H** anywhere to hold the gun up and change fittings on the fly (you stand still, the night goes on and a hit puts it away), or use **Gunsmith** at the safehouse weapon table. A refit takes 1.2 s with the gun down.
- Online the server owns XP and unlocks: it scores each night, keeps XP on the saved profile (and on the signed-in account) and refuses any fitting the profile hasn't unlocked. Solo XP is saved through the same profile when the game server is reachable, else kept in the browser.
- Supporter finishes (carbon, neon, hazard) are tied to a `supporter` entitlement on the profile, ready for the Supporter Pack. Option lists are append-only: their indices are the wire format of a build.

## Survivors

Main menu → **Survivor** opens the character creator: a lit turntable preview (drag to turn, scroll to zoom, the camera frames the part you are editing) and twelve tabs.

- Body: 10 skin tones, slim / average / broad build. Face: 6 eye shapes (round, almond, wide, narrow, sleepy, fierce), 10 eye colors, 7 facial hair styles. Hair: 14 styles, 16 colors.
- Headwear: 31 pieces in three groups. Hats (caps, beanies, bucket, cowboy, fedora, flat cap, bandana, sweatband), helmets (hard hat, army, motorcycle, football, bike, firefighter, knight) and funny hats (traffic cone, party hat, propeller cap, chef hat, top hat, viking, cooking pot, tinfoil, crown, bunny ears, pirate, sombrero, rubber duck, lampshade).
- Tops: 18, from T-shirts, tank tops, button-ups, striped, Hawaiian and flannel shirts (buttoned or open over a tee) to sweaters, hoodies and denim, leather, varsity, track, puffer, rain and camo jackets. Fabrics are drawn in the shader (plaid, stripes, camo, floral, knit, denim) with a separate accent color.
- Bottoms (7), shoes (5), gloves (4), eyewear and masks (11), neck pieces (6), back items (backpack, hiking pack, messenger bag, katana, guitar) and gear (tactical and hi-vis vests, bandolier, utility belt, fanny pack, knee pads), each with a color from a 20-color cloth palette.
- Randomize, Reset and the survivor name live in the creator header. Choices save with the profile and the slung weapon sits on top of whatever is on your back.

The look is plain data (`shared/src/config/appearance.ts`): catalog ids plus palette indices, sent as a short versioned string (`"1.4.1.2…"`) in the join options. The simulation stores the sanitized string per player and the server sends it to everyone in the room, so other players see your survivor. Catalogs and palettes are append-only: never reorder or remove entries, because indices are the wire format. Zombies are dressed from the same catalog, with muddied clothes and glowing eyes.
